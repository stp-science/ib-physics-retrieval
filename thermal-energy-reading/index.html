1. Conduction

Thermal energy transfer through matter, without bulk flow of the material.

Partner A reads · Partner B listens
📖 Read together

How does conduction transfer energy?

Conduction is the transfer of thermal energy from a hotter region to a colder region. It is most evident in solids. When one end of a solid is heated, its particles gain kinetic energy and vibrate more strongly. They transfer energy to neighbouring particles, causing them to vibrate more too. The particles stay in approximately fixed positions; they do not move along the solid.

Why are metals good conductors?

Metals are generally better thermal conductors than non-metals because free (delocalised) electrons can transfer thermal energy rapidly through the material, in addition to energy transfer through particle vibrations. Glass has no mobile free electrons available for conduction, so energy is transferred mainly through vibrations and interactions between neighbouring particles. Metals therefore generally have a higher thermal conductivity than glass under the same conditions.

Examples: The handle of a metal spoon becomes hot when the other end is placed in hot tea. A chocolate bar melts as energy transfers from your hand into it.

Remember: In conduction, thermal energy moves through the material, but the solid itself does not flow.

A explains · B challenges

Metal and glass rods are heated at one end. Explain how thermal energy travels through each rod and why the metal heats up faster.

Use the words vibrations, free electrons and glass. Partner B: ask, “Do the particles move along either rod?”

A strong explanation should include: Particles in both rods vibrate more and transfer energy to neighbouring particles. Metals also have mobile free electrons that move randomly and transfer energy rapidly. Glass lacks mobile conduction electrons, so energy is transferred mainly by vibrations and typically travels along it more slowly. Neither solid flows along the rod.

2. Convection

Thermal energy transfer through the large-scale movement of fluids.

How does convection transfer energy?

Convection transfers thermal energy through the movement of a fluid (a liquid or gas). When a region of fluid is heated, it usually expands and becomes less dense. The warmer fluid rises, while cooler, denser fluid sinks to take its place. This movement creates a convection current.

Where does convection happen?

Convection helps produce sea breezes: air warmed over land rises and cooler air moves in from over the sea. Convection also occurs in the Earth's mantle, where hot rock can move very slowly over long timescales.

Remember: In convection, the fluid itself moves and carries thermal energy with it.

Discuss · Explain it to your partner

B explains · A challenges

Explain why warmer water rises and cooler water sinks when a container of water is heated from below.

Use the words temperature, density and convection current. Partner A: ask what moves.

A strong explanation should include: Water at the bottom warms, expands and becomes less dense, so it rises. Cooler, denser water sinks to replace it. This forms a convection current that transfers thermal energy.

3. Thermal radiation

Energy transfer by electromagnetic radiation — no material medium required.

How does thermal radiation transfer energy?

Thermal radiation is the transfer of energy by electromagnetic radiation. The energy is carried in packets called photons. Unlike conduction and convection, radiation does not need a material medium. It can travel through the vacuum of space.

Examples: Energy from the Sun reaches and warms the Earth. You can also feel thermal radiation from a campfire without touching the flames.

Remember: Thermal radiation can transfer energy even when there are no particles between the source and the object being heated.

Discuss · Explain it to your partner

A explains · B challenges

Explain how the Sun transfers energy to Earth even though space is nearly a vacuum.

Use the words electromagnetic radiation, photons and vacuum. Partner B: ask whether a medium is needed.

A strong explanation should include: The Sun emits electromagnetic radiation, which transfers energy as photons. It can travel through a vacuum, so no material medium is required.
