01 / THE BIG PICTURE · 8 MINUTES

Sunlight in. Thermal radiation out.

The surface absorbs energy and warms. It then emits its own radiation. Reflection and thermal emission are different processes.

Keep these two types of radiation separate.

The hot Sun emits ultraviolet, visible light and infrared, with its emission peaking in the visible region. Earth is much cooler and emits mainly long-wavelength infrared. Some incoming solar infrared is absorbed in the atmosphere, but the main greenhouse mechanism concerns infrared emitted by Earth.

FOLLOW THE ENERGY

One system, six processes

Select a process to highlight its pathway and read the explanation.
A

Why doesn’t Earth just cool down?

Earth continuously loses energy as radiation, but it also continuously receives energy from the Sun. At radiative equilibrium, the total absorbed solar power equals the total thermal power emitted to space. Its average temperature is then steady.

Think of a bath: a tap adds water and a drain removes it. Equal flow rates keep the water level steady. Here, water represents stored energy and water level represents temperature.

Pabsorbed = Pemitted to space

More power in than out → warming. More power out than in → cooling. Greenhouse gases slow energy loss at a given surface temperature; the system warms until outgoing power balances absorbed power again.

B

Natural and enhanced greenhouse effects

The natural greenhouse effect keeps the surface and lower atmosphere warmer than they would otherwise be. A simple radiating-Earth calculation gives an effective temperature of about 255 K; a commonly used approximate mean surface temperature is 288 K.

These are different quantities: 255 K is an effective radiating temperature, not the actual surface temperature. A simple model with albedo but no greenhouse warming predicts too cold a surface.

The enhanced greenhouse effect occurs when human activities raise greenhouse-gas concentrations. Initially, less infrared escapes to space for the same temperature profile, so the Earth system gains energy and warms.

Physics link: albedo, emissivity and the factor of four

Earth intercepts sunlight over its disc area, πR², but emits radiation over its spherical area, 4πR². Averaging incoming solar power over the globe gives S/4. If α is albedo, the absorbed average is (1 − α)S/4.

(1 − α)S / 4 = εσT⁴

This is a simple radiating-body model. Taking ε = 1 assumes black-body thermal emission; it does not mean “all absorbed energy is immediately re-emitted”. Albedo concerns reflected sunlight; the emissivity here concerns outgoing thermal infrared. The real surface–atmosphere system requires more detail.

Half the globe is sunlit at a time. The intercepting disc area is a quarter of the total spherical surface area; this does not mean only a quarter of the surface is illuminated.

02 / MOLECULAR EVIDENCE · 8 MINUTES

What makes a greenhouse gas?

Investigate how different molecules interact with radiation using PhET: Molecules and Light.

Read this before you test

Molecules have discrete energy levels. A photon can be absorbed if its energy matches an allowed difference between molecular energy levels: E = hf = ΔE.

For greenhouse gases, particular infrared frequencies excite vibrations, often with rotational changes. In the resonance model, the radiation’s oscillating electric field transfers energy to an appropriate molecular vibration. An infrared-active vibration changes the molecule’s electric dipole moment.

Collisions can transfer this energy to other molecules, increasing the air’s thermal energy. Molecules can also emit infrared photons. Atmospheric infrared emission goes in all directions; it is not mirror reflection.

N₂ and O₂ do not significantly absorb Earth’s thermal infrared through these vibrational modes. Abundance alone does not make a gas a greenhouse gas.

03 / WHERE THE GASES COME FROM · 5 MINUTES

Same gases. Different sources.

Make your own table with the four core gases, their formulae, at least one natural source and at least one human source or influence. Explain water vapour’s special role.

CO₂Carbon dioxideRespiration and decomposition; release from oceans; volcanic activity.Burning coal, oil and gas; cement production; forest clearance and burning, which can also reduce carbon uptake.
CH₄MethaneMicrobes decomposing organic matter without oxygen in wetlands; termites.Livestock digestion, especially ruminants; manure storage; rice paddies; landfills; leaks during fossil-fuel extraction and supply.
N₂ONitrous oxideMicrobial processes in soils and oceans as part of the nitrogen cycle.Nitrogen fertilisers and manure increase microbial emissions from soils; some industrial processes and combustion.
H₂OWater vapourEvaporation from oceans, lakes and soil; transpiration from plants.Combustion and irrigation add water locally. The major global climate influence is indirect: human-caused warming increases atmospheric water vapour, amplifying warming.

Water vapour is mainly a feedback

As temperature rises, evaporation can increase and the atmosphere can contain more water vapour. More water vapour strengthens infrared absorption and amplifies the initial warming. Condensation and precipitation remove it relatively quickly, so direct human water-vapour emissions are not the main driver of long-term global warming.

Initial warming → more water vapour → stronger greenhouse effect → additional warming

Other greenhouse gases

Ozone, O₃: occurs naturally through atmospheric chemistry. Near the ground, reactions involving pollutants from vehicles and industry can increase ozone. It is formed in the air rather than simply emitted as ozone.

Halogenated gases: examples include CFCs and HFCs from manufactured refrigerants and industrial uses. These examples have predominantly human sources; a natural source does not have to exist for every greenhouse gas.

Clouds are droplets and/or ice crystals, not water vapour. They influence both reflection of sunlight and infrared radiation.

Rehearse with these questions

Does visible light bounce off Earth and turn into infrared?
No. Reflected visible light remains visible. Absorbed radiation transfers energy to the surface; the warm surface then emits its own thermal infrared radiation.
Do greenhouse gases absorb all radiation?
No. Absorption depends on wavelength and allowed molecular energy transitions. Much visible sunlight passes through, and some outgoing infrared also escapes.
Does “trapped” mean energy can never escape?
No. Energy still escapes to space. Greenhouse gases reduce outgoing radiation for a given temperature profile. A warmer equilibrium restores the balance between absorbed solar power and outgoing thermal power.
Is the atmosphere a mirror reflecting infrared?
No. Greenhouse gases absorb infrared and the atmosphere emits infrared in all directions. Collisions also exchange energy between molecules. This is different from mirror reflection.
