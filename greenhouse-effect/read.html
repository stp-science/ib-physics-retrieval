01 / THE BIG PICTURE · 8 MINUTES

Sunlight in. Thermal radiation out.

The surface absorbs energy and warms. It then emits its own radiation. Reflection and thermal emission are different processes.

Keep these two types of radiation separate.

The hot Sun emits ultraviolet, visible light and infrared, with its emission peaking in the visible region. Earth is much cooler and emits mainly long-wavelength infrared. Some incoming solar infrared is absorbed in the atmosphere, but the main greenhouse mechanism concerns infrared emitted by Earth.

Where does the reflected arrow start?

In this diagram it starts at a cloud: cloud droplets reflect some sunlight back to space. Earth’s surface also reflects some sunlight. Neither reflection turns visible light into infrared. The infrared arrows start separately at the warm surface.

Infrared arrives

An infrared photon reaches a greenhouse-gas molecule. It can be absorbed if it has the right energy.

The molecule vibrates more

The photon is absorbed and transfers its energy. The molecule’s atoms move relative to one another as bonds stretch or bend.

↖ ● ↘

Energy moves on

Collisions transfer energy to other molecules. Infrared can also be emitted in any direction, including towards the surface.

The moving molecule illustrates vibration, not a change in the whole molecule’s position. It is a schematic, not to scale.
