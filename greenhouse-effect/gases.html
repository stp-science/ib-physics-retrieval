03 / WHERE THE GASES COME FROM · 5 MINUTES

Same gases. Different sources.

Make your own table with the four core gases, their formulae, at least one natural source and at least one human source or influence. Explain water vapour’s special role.

Core greenhouse gasNatural sourcesHuman sources or influence
CO₂Carbon dioxideRespiration and decomposition; release from oceans; volcanic activity.Burning coal, oil and gas; cement production; forest clearance and burning, which can also reduce carbon uptake.
CH₄MethaneMicrobes decomposing organic matter without oxygen in wetlands; termites.Livestock digestion, especially ruminants; manure storage; rice paddies; landfills; leaks during fossil-fuel extraction and supply.
N₂ONitrous oxideMicrobial processes in soils and oceans as part of the nitrogen cycle.Nitrogen fertilisers and manure increase microbial emissions from soils; some industrial processes and combustion.
H₂OWater vapourEvaporation from oceans, lakes and soil; transpiration from plants.Combustion and irrigation add water locally. The major global climate influence is indirect: human-caused warming increases atmospheric water vapour, amplifying warming.

Water vapour is mainly a feedback

As temperature rises, evaporation can increase and the atmosphere can contain more water vapour. More water vapour strengthens infrared absorption and amplifies the initial warming. Condensation and precipitation remove it relatively quickly, so direct human water-vapour emissions are not the main driver of long-term global warming.

Initial warming → more water vapour → stronger greenhouse effect → additional warming

Other greenhouse gases

Ozone, O₃: occurs naturally through atmospheric chemistry. Near the ground, reactions involving pollutants from vehicles and industry can increase ozone. It is formed in the air rather than simply emitted as ozone.

Halogenated gases: examples include CFCs and HFCs from manufactured refrigerants and industrial uses. These examples have predominantly human sources; a natural source does not have to exist for every greenhouse gas.

Clouds are droplets and/or ice crystals, not water vapour. They influence both reflection of sunlight and infrared radiation.
