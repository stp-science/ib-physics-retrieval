# Question bank review — 3 November 2026

Reviewed the 1,000 published entries, including every original conceptual pair and every numerical template and its generated cases. The bank retains 664 common SL/HL entries and 336 additional-HL entries across the 24 syllabus subtopics and a separate skills collection.

## Outcome

527 entries have revised question text, model answers, or numerical presentation. Of these, 352 have changed question text. There are now 122 questions with suggested practice marks, including structured calculations, interpretation of supplied data and short explanatory responses, alongside five four-option multiple-choice questions. Original question IDs and topic counts are preserved.

Short recall and direct calculations are retained where appropriate: they support retrieval and can represent individual examination subparts. This is an original retrieval bank, not a mock paper, an official IB question bank, or a claim to reproduce the balance of a complete examination. Full papers also require sustained multi-part reasoning and interpretation of actual diagrams and graphs. The page links to the official specimen pack for that purpose.

## Principal corrections

- Work–energy theorem: net work equals change in kinetic energy.
- Static friction: distinguish its limiting value from its adjustable actual value.
- Impulse: distinguish constant-force multiplication from the general time integral.
- Torque and angular acceleration: include perpendicular distance and direction/axis conditions.
- Reference frames: supply origin, axis and timing conventions in transformation problems.
- Thermal physics: distinguish ideal-gas molecular kinetic energy from general internal energy; explain phase changes at constant pressure; retain Q for thermal energy.
- Conduction: distinguish positive heat-transfer rate from a signed temperature gradient.
- Gas and thermodynamic processes: identify fixed amount, constant temperature and reversible/quasistatic assumptions where needed; clarify signed heat and work, and cycle orientation.
- Interference: require in-phase sources for the stated path-difference rules; give angle conventions, normal incidence and small-angle assumptions where needed.
- Standing waves: specify open/closed pipe ends and neglected end corrections.
- Doppler effect: use signed wavelength shifts and opposite frequency-shift signs; replace a question about syllabus restrictions with a physics explanation.
- Fields: distinguish external work, field work and potential-energy change; make gradient problems linear; identify an empty conducting cavity.
- Induction: supply the rod and rotation-axis orientations; distinguish instantaneous and average emf; eliminate non-zero floating-point residues for cos 90°.
- Quantum and nuclear physics: clarify binding energy per nucleon, photon energy transfer, expected radioactive populations and a steady versus growing chain reaction.
- Stellar parallax: define the parallax angle as half the total six-month displacement.
- Skills: add linearisation, graph gradients/intercepts, background-corrected half-life, uncertainty of gradients, evidence-based conclusions and limits of error-bar comparisons.

## Validation

`node scripts/verify-bank.cjs` checks the generated bank, unique IDs and prompts, course counts, HL-only restrictions, answer subparts, MC options, numerical notation and selected physics regressions. An optional jsdom path enables an additional pass through all 1,000 entries in the application: start, render, reveal, self-mark and review. Browser preview checks cover the original layout and the smaller type size and line breaks for longer structured questions.

Calculated model answers generally retain three significant figures as a checking aid. Students should use the precision justified by the supplied data; equivalent appropriately rounded answers are acceptable. Suggested marks are authored for these original practice items, not official IB mark allocations. Self-marking remains “I knew this” / “Revisit this”, rather than an automatic exam score.
